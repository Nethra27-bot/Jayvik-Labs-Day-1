# Jayvik-Labs-Day-1



1. FIVE IMPORTANT ASSETS

1. Employee Email Accounts: Used for business communication, customer inquiries, invoices, and password-reset requests. Compromised accounts could expose sensitive information or enable fraud.

2. Shared Computers: Used by eight employees to access business applications and company data. These devices may store login sessions, downloaded files, and confidential information.

3. Company Website: Represents BrightLayer Stores online and may support customer inquiries, product information, and business transactions. Website disruption or compromise could damage reputation and customer trust.

4. Cloud Storage with Invoices: Stores financial records, supplier details, customer information, and transaction documents. Loss or unauthorized disclosure could cause financial and operational problems.

5. User Passwords and Public Wi-Fi Router: Passwords protect access to company systems, while the Wi-Fi router provides network connectivity. Weak passwords or insecure wireless settings could provide attackers with unauthorized access.

2. THREE POTENTIAL THREATS

1. Phishing Attackers: Cybercriminals may send fraudulent emails to employees, attempting to steal login credentials or trick them into opening malicious attachments.

2. Unauthorized Users or External Attackers: An outsider or unauthorized person could exploit an unattended shared computer, weak account protection, or insecure public Wi-Fi to access company resources.

3. Malicious Website Attackers: Cybercriminals may exploit weaknesses in the company website to modify content, steal information, or disrupt business operations.

3. THREE VULNERABILITIES

1. Weak Authentication: Employees may use short, reused passwords or accounts without multifactor authentication (MFA), making unauthorized access easier.

2. Insufficient Shared-Computer Security: Shared devices may retain logged-in sessions, saved passwords, or downloaded sensitive files. Automatic screen locking and individual user accounts may be missing.

3. Inadequate Website and Wi-Fi Security Configuration: The website may use outdated software or plugins, while the Wi-Fi router may have weak credentials, outdated firmware, or insecure configuration. These weaknesses could expose business systems to attack.

Note: These are potential weaknesses to verify during an actual assessment; the scenario does not confirm that they exist.

5. SECURITY RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Enable MFA: Require multifactor authentication for employee email and cloud storage accounts. Enforce strong, unique passwords and use a password manager where appropriate.
Mapped risk: Phishing-related account takeover and invoice fraud.

2. Secure Shared Computers: Create individual employee accounts, enable automatic screen locking, disable unnecessary password saving, and require users to sign out after each session. Restrict administrator privileges.
Mapped risk: Unauthorized access and data leakage from shared devices.

3. Protect the Company Website: Keep the content management system, plugins, and server software updated. Enforce HTTPS, remove unused plugins, restrict administrative access, and maintain regular backups.
Mapped risk: Website compromise, information exposure, and service disruption.

4. Secure the Wi-Fi Router: Change default administrator credentials, use WPA3 or WPA2-AES security, update firmware, and disable WPS if unnecessary. Keep guest Wi-Fi separate from business devices and resources.
Mapped risk: Unauthorized network access and exposure of internal resources.

5. Train Employees Against Phishing: Teach employees to verify suspicious links, attachments, payment requests, and invoice changes. Establish a simple process for reporting suspicious emails.
Mapped risk: Credential theft, email compromise, and fraudulent payments.

6. Protect Cloud Data: Apply least-privilege access, review permissions regularly, enable version history where available, and maintain tested backups of important invoices.
Mapped risk: Unauthorized disclosure, accidental deletion, and loss of financial records.

7. Monitor and Review Security: Review account sign-in alerts, website logs, and router administration activity. Investigate unusual activity and document incidents and corrective actions.
Mapped risk: Delayed detection of account compromise, website attacks, or unauthorized network access.
